To determine which AI model is "better," the industry uses a multi-layered evaluation framework. As of 2026, the comparison goes far beyond simple accuracy. It is now divided into Technical Intelligence, Operational Efficiency, Agentic Capability, and Human Preference.  
Here is the breakdown of the criteria and features used to rate modern models like GPT-5 and Gemini.
1. Intelligence & Reasoning Benchmarks
These are the "SATs" for AI. They measure raw cognitive ability across different subjects.  
GPQA (Graduate-Level Google-Proof Q&A): Currently considered the "gold standard" for frontier models. It uses biology, physics, and chemistry questions written by PhDs that are designed to be impossible to answer via a simple Google search.  
MMLU-Pro: An advanced version of the classic Massive Multitask Language Understanding. It uses 10-choice questions (instead of 4) to reduce "lucky guessing" and focuses on complex reasoning rather than rote memorization.  
Humanity's Last Exam (HLE): A high-tier benchmark designed to test expert-level reasoning that pushes the absolute limits of current AI.  
2. Coding & Agentic Capabilities
In 2026, a model isn't just rated on writing code, but on its ability to act as a software engineer or assistant.
SWE-bench Verified: Measures if the AI can actually fix real-world bugs in GitHub repositories.
